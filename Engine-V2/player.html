Playing solo: the basics

The tabletop game, played alone. You are the responder. An Incident Master (an AI model, or the built-in offline clue tables) has hidden an attack chain on the board and answers your investigation with hints instead of a GM.

  1. Four phases are hidden: Initial Compromise, Pivot & Escalate, C2 & Exfil and Persistence. Your job is to name all four.
  2. Pick a procedure, then roll the d20. Every roll spends one turn, and 11 or higher succeeds.
  3. The roll's quality sets the clue's quality. 11–14 is a partial success: an indirect, suggestive read rather than a conclusive one. 15+ gives clear evidence, and a natural 20 all but names the technique. Below 11 fails, and the turn is simply gone.
  4. A success earns the chain. Procedure detect certain phases. When one of yours covers a hidden phase, that card flips face-up and the technique is named, partial successes included.
  5. Rolling with no card selected still gets you a phase-level hint, but no named technique, and it still spends the turn.
  6. Accuse when ready. Name all four cards to close the case. A wrong accusation costs a strike.

You have 10 turns and 3 strikes. The dice, the turn counter and the scoring all work exactly as they do on the tabletop. The only thing that changes is that the chain starts face-down and the Incident Master answers.
